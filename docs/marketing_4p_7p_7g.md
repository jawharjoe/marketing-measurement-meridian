# From 4Ps to 7Ps to the proposed 7Gs

The marketing mix **strategy** frameworks on this page are distinct from statistical **Marketing Mix Modeling (MMM)**. The framework discussed below is a research proposal, not an accepted causal measurement standard.

## The original 4Ps

| P | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Product | What the company offers to meet a need | Running shoes |
| Price | How the product is priced and what consumers pay | $120 list price |
| Place | Where and how customers can obtain it | Store, marketplace, website |
| Promotion | How the offering is communicated | Search ads, video ads, promotions |

## The service-oriented 7Ps

The traditional framework adds **People** (those involved in delivery and service), **Process** (the journey and procedures), and **Physical Evidence** (tangible signals of quality, such as an environment, packaging or a receipt). These additions highlight the experience around the product.

## The proposed 7Gs: generative AI changes the interaction

Makrydakis and colleagues propose new components for AI-mediated marketing, rather than offering a validated statistical MMM replacement.

| Proposed G | Simple meaning | Question for a marketing scientist |
|---|---|---|
| Generative Offer Models | Offers can be generated/adapted | Do dynamic offers increase *incremental* purchases? |
| Generative Value Calibration | AI adapts perceived value and pricing | Is uplift due to useful value or unfair targeting? |
| Generative Access Architectures | AI shapes discovery and accessibility | What ranking effects increase or limit choice? |
| Generative Persuasion Systems | AI adapts communications and messaging | Is persuasion accurate, respectful and effective? |
| Generative Agency Networks | People and AI agents act together | Who is responsible for a poor recommendation? |
| Generative Experience Flows | Customer journeys change in real time | How do we measure quality of the full journey? |
| Generative Trust Signals | Trust depends on credibility and authenticity | Do hallucinations or missing context damage trust? |

## Why this paper is useful to discuss

The paper offers a vocabulary for thinking about AI-mediated influence, but raises difficult measurement problems: personalization quality, trust, fairness, transparency, accountability, causal impact, and long-term customer value. An LLM can generate messages; that is not proof it improves outcomes. Better research needs carefully designed A/B or geo experiments, behavioral evidence, and quality/safety metrics alongside conversion metrics.

The authors discuss a cross-sectional, self-reported design; findings should not be taken as definitive evidence that the 7G framework improves business outcomes. The reading is a **discussion starter**, not a benchmark or implementation recipe.

**Source:** Makrydakis, N. S., Spiliotopoulos, D., Michalakelis, C., and Lymperi, A. (2026), *Marketing Mix Rebuilding for the Age of Generative AI: From 7P to 7G Marketing Mix Model*. [ResearchGate publication](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/399671440_Marketing_mix_rebuilding_for_the_age_of_generative_AI_From_7P_to_7G_marketing_mix_model).

**Closing question:** *How do we prove AI-generated marketing creates real value without reducing trust?*
